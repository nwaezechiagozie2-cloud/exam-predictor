💡 How Scoring & Trend Intelligence Works Click to expand

Exam forecasting is grounded in structural curriculum design and examiner psychology. Unlike objective multiple-choice exams that can touch every subtopic lightly, written theory examinations have limited question slots (often 5 to 10 problems) worth 100 marks total.

1. Magnitude & Recency Decay

For each topic-year, we calculate magnitude: marks if marks > 0 else question_count. Years are sorted chronologically and weighted with an exponential recency decay w[i] = 0.7^(n-1-i), where the most recent year receives a weight of 1.0. The base score is the weighted average.

2. Trend Classification & Multipliers

Examiners alternate or maintain key topics systematically:
• Staple (1.0x): Tested every single year — foundational to syllabus.
• Rising (1.3x): Marks strictly climbing — examiner's growing focus.
• Alternating-Due (1.4x): Every-other-year rhythm, skipped last cycle — high probability of returning!
• Emerging (1.15x): Absent previously, introduced recently.
• Alternating-Rest (0.4x): Every-other-year rhythm, tested last cycle — likely resting.
• Fading (0.5x): Tested historically, dropped from recent paper.
